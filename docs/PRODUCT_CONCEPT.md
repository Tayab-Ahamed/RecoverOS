# RecoverOS product concept: the Revenue Observatory

RecoverOS is not presented as another dashboard of cards and tables. It is a **Revenue Observatory**: a control room that watches a merchant's revenue field, sees which money is drifting out of orbit, and dispatches the smallest compliant intervention that can bring it back.

## The visual metaphor

The main surface is a living **Recovery Radar**. Each case is a signal in a revenue field. Its position communicates priority, its color communicates state, and its pulse communicates urgency. Selecting a signal opens the decision story: what happened, what the agent believes, what it considered, what policy allowed or refused, and what payment evidence closed the loop.

The metaphor is functional, not decorative. It turns the core product claim into one glance:

> Revenue is not a list of failed transactions. It is a field of recoverable opportunities with different urgency, value, consent, and intervention risk.

## The product language

Use short, operational language rather than generic AI language. Prefer **Signal**, **Decision**, **Guardrail**, **Proof**, **Recovered**, **Held**, **Escalated**, and **No contact**. Avoid phrases such as “AI-powered dashboard,” “smart automation,” or “insights.”

## The demo arc

The five-minute demo follows one signal from drift to proof. The reviewer first sees the batch money field, then selects one high-value signal to see the adaptive proposal, then opens the opt-out signal to see the system refuse contact, then opens the approval-held signal to see bounded autonomy, and finally opens the Proof Lab to compare adaptive planning against the fixed baseline.

## Design principles

The interface should feel closer to a premium observability product than a CRM. It should use a dark instrument-panel surface, one electric blue for active signals, green only for verified money, amber for human decisions, and red only for policy or provider risk. Every visual flourish must have a semantic job. The page should be usable without animation, keyboard navigable, and responsive on a laptop screen.
